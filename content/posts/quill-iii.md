<h2 class="wp-block-heading">China Unveils an Armed "Robot Wolf" Built on Nvidia Hardware</h2>

<p>China has publicly revealed the AS2-W, a quad-wheeled robot running on Nvidia-powered systems, just weeks after an armed version of the same platform surfaced during joint military drills in Mongolia. The design trades legs for wheels, giving it a lower profile and faster ground speed than the legged military robots we've covered before, while still handling rough terrain reasonably well. What makes this one notable isn't just the hardware, it's the timing: unveiling the civilian-facing version so soon after the armed variant appeared in drills suggests China is comfortable blurring the line between its commercial and military robotics programs in public view, rather than keeping the two tracks separate.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Google Earth's AI Deepfake Tool Didn't Survive Its First Day</h2>

<p>Google briefly launched a feature inside Google Earth that let users edit satellite imagery using AI text prompts, effectively letting anyone generate a convincing "photo" of any location doing whatever the prompt described. It was pulled within 24 hours. The speed of the reversal says a lot on its own, satellite imagery carries an unusual amount of implicit trust since people rarely think to question what a photo from orbit is telling them, and a tool that can quietly reshape that trust turns out to be much easier to build than it is to responsibly ship.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">India's App Economy Is Shifting From Downloads to Real Spending</h2>

<p>India's app market posted a record $345 million in revenue last quarter, and the more interesting detail isn't the total, it's the shift in behavior underneath it. For years, India has been characterized as a download-heavy, spend-light market: massive install numbers, thin monetization. That equation appears to be changing, with users increasingly willing to pay for subscriptions and in-app purchases rather than sticking to ad-supported free tiers. For a market this size, even a modest shift in willingness to pay reshapes how global app makers prioritize the region.</p>
